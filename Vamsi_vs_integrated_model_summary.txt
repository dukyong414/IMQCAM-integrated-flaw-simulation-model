Summary: Vamsi's Raptor setup and the integrated model
Date of the discussion: Saturday, October 31, 2026
Subject: how Subraveti / Vamsi's published Raptor runs differ from the integrated defect model

This note summarizes that session. Vamsi's case is the velocity study in Subraveti et al.: Raptor on a 0.5 mm cube. The integrated model is the 5 x 5 x 14 mm pillar (350 mm3) that also carries CMU gas and CMU lack-of-fusion, with Raptor lack-of-fusion used in place of the CMU lack-of-fusion term.


1. What both use

- Hatch 140 um, layer thickness 30 um, rotation 67 degrees.
- Power and the Vamsi single-track width files (ULI_v####_theta*_widths.txt).
- The width spectrum is the average of the eight track orientations, not a single theta0 file.
- Lambda shifts only the mean width, depth, and cap. The measured fluctuations are left unscaled.
- Lambda = 1.04 is the upper defect bound. Lambda = 1.14 is the lower bound. The older x1.15 width scale was dropped because it is not the paper's factor.
- The Raptor call itself is the stock one: ScanPathBuilder, create_melt_pool, compute_porosity, and the stock Numba melt mask.
- Vamsi's width files have a time axis 1000 times too short. Time is multiplied by 1000 so the axis is in seconds, matching the official file.


2. Where they differ

Geometry
- Vamsi: 0.5 mm cube. Reported samples are 1000 cubes (125 mm3) from 1300 to 1550 mm/s, and 250 cubes (31.25 mm3) from 1600 to 1700 mm/s. The paper's own calibration ensembles were sized to the CT volume (336 mm3 at 1550 mm/s).
- Integrated model: one 5 x 5 x 14 mm pillar, 350 mm3.

Voxel size
- Vamsi: 5 um.
- Integrated model, before this session's last rerun: about 10.7 um. Raptor keeps a defect only when it fills at least two voxels, so that coarse grid dropped gaps smaller than about 20 um.
- After the rerun: the pillar also uses 5 um (2,806,604,801 voxels), so those gaps are kept.

How lambda is applied
- Vamsi: lambda multiplies the mean width, depth, and cap only.
- Integrated model, earlier column: lambda multiplied the whole spectrum, so the fluctuations grew by the same factor (4% at lambda = 1.04).
- After the rerun: the pillar uses the mean-only shift, same as the paper. At 1300 mm/s the mean width goes from 172.7 um to 179.6 um.

Scan extension
- Vamsi's cube: tracks extend half a cube past each face (0.25 mm).
- Integrated pillar: ScanPathBuilder extends by the longest edge of the build (14 mm). That was left as-is when the voxel size and the mean-only lambda were matched to the cube.

Pore-size cutoff
- Vamsi's reported comparison with experiment keeps pores of equivalent diameter 20 um and larger.
- On his 0.5 mm cube, the integrated model was first counted with every Raptor pore, including those under 20 um. At 1450 mm/s and lambda = 1.04 that was 59 pores, all smaller than 20 um (largest 16.6 um), or 0.472 pores/mm3. After the cutoff that row is 0, and the cube column matches the paper replication.
- The pillar column is a different count. Lack-of-fusion pores whose centers lie within 0.5 mm of an outer face are removed. That skin is not applied to the 0.5 mm cube, because the cube is only 0.5 mm across and the skin would erase the whole domain.

Other defect sources
- Vamsi's Raptor result is lack-of-fusion only.
- The integrated pillar also lists CMU gas and CMU lack-of-fusion. In the combined prediction, Raptor lack-of-fusion replaces CMU lack-of-fusion. Gas pores drawn at 1300-1450 mm/s on the pillar are the CMU gas term, not Raptor.


3. What that did to the numbers

Units below are pores per mm3. Counts in parentheses are pores in that domain.

Why the pillar looked porous at 1300-1600 mm/s before the matched rerun
- From 1300 through 1550 mm/s the coarse pillar was fully melted, so it had no Raptor lack-of-fusion. At 1600 mm/s the only unmelted marks were 4 single voxels, which Raptor drops.
- The points visible at 1300-1450 mm/s were CMU gas pores. Raptor lack-of-fusion on that coarse pillar started at 1650 mm/s (6 pores) and 1700 mm/s (57 pores).

After matching the cube's voxel size and mean-only lambda
- 1300-1400 mm/s stay at 0 on the pillar. The mean pool (about 170-180 um) still covers the 140 um hatch, and every voxel is melted.
- Interior Raptor lack-of-fusion on the pillar starts at 1450 mm/s (18 pores, 0.051 /mm3). The mean pool there is 160.5 um, then narrows to 145.1 um at 1700 mm/s.
- 1650 and 1700 mm/s rose from 0.003 and 0.014 /mm3 on the coarse grid to 5.771 (2020 pores) and 14.346 (5021 pores). Those gaps had been smaller than a coarse voxel and were being discarded.
- 1500 mm/s on the pillar is 0.011 /mm3 (4 pores), next to 0.016 /mm3 (2 pores) on the paper cube at the same speed.

Lambda = 1.14 on the pillar (mean pool 14% wider)
- Fully melted through 1600 mm/s (width used at 1600 mm/s is 169 um).
- Pores start at 1650 mm/s (5 pores, 0.014 /mm3) and 1700 mm/s (58 pores, 0.166 /mm3).

Paper's own stated values
- Subraveti et al. give one rate for the fatigue window, not a value at each speed. For pores of 20-30 um they report 1-3 pores per 200 mm3, which is 0.005-0.015 pores/mm3, from 1300 through 1500 mm/s.
- At lambda = 1.14 they report no stochastic lack-of-fusion at 1300, 1350, and 1400 mm/s.
- At lambda = 1.04 the volume fraction in that window is on the order of 10^-5 percent. At 1300 mm/s they describe the material as 99.99999% dense. Volume fraction is not pores/mm3.
- 1550 mm/s is the calibration speed (336 mm3). Largest pores in those ensembles are 65-83 um at lambda = 1.04 and 33-39 um at lambda = 1.14. 1600-1700 mm/s are outside the paper. Fig. 6 is a log plot and does not list a number at each speed.

5. Bottom line

Once voxel size, mean-only lambda, the averaged spectrum, and the 20 um cutoff are aligned, the integrated model on Vamsi's 0.5 mm cube follows his Raptor result. The integrated 5 x 5 x 14 mm pillar is still a different prediction: a much larger build, a 14 mm scan extension instead of 0.25 mm, and a 0.5 mm surface skin removed from the lack-of-fusion count. That is why the pillar densities sit above the cube from 1450 mm/s upward, and why lambda = 1.14 stays empty on the cube until a single pore at 1700 mm/s while the pillar already has 5 pores at 1650 mm/s.
